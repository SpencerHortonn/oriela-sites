## 3. NEWSLETTER DRAFT

**Subject:** The playground strategy

In 2004, spammers were winning. They'd crack one blog's defenses, then move to the next, and no single site could keep up.

So Matt Mullenweg's team did something unusual: they got every WordPress site to fight back together. "We created a system that essentially allowed all the kids being bullied on the playground to work together and to gang up," he told Shane Parrish on The Knowledge Project. Spammers adapted in hours — and the network adapted in real time.

That became Akismet. And it's also the purest expression of Mullenweg's worldview: "I believe all proprietary software to be an evolutionary dead end." When everyone works on a shared resource, you get the opposite of the tragedy of the commons — each person acting in self-interest makes the thing better.

Three operating principles from the episode worth adopting:

**Decide fast on reversible calls.** "Make reversible decisions quickly and irreversible ones deliberately." Most decisions are reversible. You'll learn more in a week of doing than a month of debating.

**Capture less than you create.** Mullenweg's rule for commercializing open source: take enough to sustain the business, always less than the value you create for others. It's why the ecosystem keeps growing instead of resenting you.

**Invest in trust deliberately.** Trust = communication × time. Automattic flies 1,500 people together 3–4 times a year because breaking bread builds what video calls can't.

The pattern across all three: systems that compound beat efforts that don't.
