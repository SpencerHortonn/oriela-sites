## 1. SHOW NOTES

### Title
Marc Benioff on the Beginner's Mind, Steve Jobs, and Betting Big on AI Agents

### Summary
Salesforce co-founder and CEO Marc Benioff joins Lenny to unpack how he built the world's second-largest software company while keeping a startup mentality. They dig into his early programming days, the lessons Steve Jobs taught him, how Salesforce weathered layoffs and hard times, and why Benioff believes AI agents are the defining technology of our lifetime.

### 3 Key Takeaways
1. **Keep the beginner's mindset at any scale.** Benioff describes Salesforce as "a 25-year-old, 75,000-person, $350 billion market cap startup." Stay curious, stay open, and never believe you have all the answers.
2. **Your customers are your best salespeople.** Share their stories — how they're using your product and the results they're seeing — across your marketing, especially for high-profile clients.
3. **AI agents will absorb repetitive work so humans can do human work.** In health care, for example, an agent could handle simple reminders and data entry, freeing professionals to focus on deeper, more empathetic interactions.

## 3. NEWSLETTER DRAFT

**Subject:** The $350B startup mindset

Marc Benioff runs the second-largest software company in the world. 75,000 employees. A market cap north of $300 billion.

And he insists it's a startup.

"A 25-year-old, 75,000-person, $350 billion market cap startup," as he put it on Lenny's Podcast this week. That's not a sound bite — it's an operating system. The beginner's mindset means staying curious when you could coast, staying open when you think you know, and evolving when you're already winning.

Benioff borrowed the perfect image for it from Toyota: "Better, better, better, never best." In a crowded market, you're forced to innovate. Alone at the top, stagnation sets in — unless you manufacture the hunger yourself.

Two more ideas from the episode worth stealing:

**Your customers are your best salespeople.** Not your ad budget, not your brand campaign. Tell customer stories — how they use the product, the results they see — and put them everywhere, especially the high-profile ones.

**Give before you get.** Benioff's relationship with Steve Jobs worked because it was built on generosity and mutual respect, not transactions. The more you give — advice, help, introductions — the more comes back, in opportunities and partnerships you can't plan for.

The through line: success doesn't come from arriving. It comes from refusing to believe you've arrived.
