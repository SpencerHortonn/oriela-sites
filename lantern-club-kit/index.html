The idea

Put the phones away. Get the night back.

"Every night has one rule: the phone stays in the pouch. Everything else is clay, conversation, music, and people who showed up to actually be there."

That's not a willpower problem. It's a design problem. The phone is engineered to win every contest for your attention, and it does, every time.

Lantern Club is my answer. One night a month, phones go in the pouch at the door, and for two hours you're just a person again. Making things. Talking. The way it used to feel before every room had a screen in it.

I'm Spencer. I'm 18. I started Lantern Club because the best nights I've had never involved a screen, and I wanted more of them. Turns out I'm not the only one.

Spencer

How a night works

Four steps. Then just talk.

Doors 6:30
Step 01

Phones in the pouch

You walk in, your phone goes in the canvas pouch at the door. It stays there all night. That's the whole rule.

7:00
Step 02

Make something

Every night has a hands-on activity. Clay, painting, building, cooking. Zero experience needed. Bad art encouraged.

7:45
Step 03

Draw a card

Between rounds we pull conversation cards. Deep ones, funny ones, ones that start real stories. Everyone answers.

9:00
Step 04

Talk until you're done

No agenda after that. Most people stay the full two hours and don't want to leave. Phones back at the door on your way out.
